Here are the key deadlines for your business insurance policies – plus the tricks that really help in practice.

## Deadlines at a Glance

| Insurance | Typical Deadline | Special Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Liability, Property Insurance | 3 months before contract expiry | Special cancellation after a claim (2 weeks) or premium increase |
| Occupational Pension (BVG) | 6 months, usually by June 30 | Workforce consent mandatory |
| Accident Insurance (UVG) | 3 months before the end of the insurance year | Seamless transition to new insurer required |
| Daily Sickness Benefits Insurance | 3 months before contract expiry | When switching: watch for ongoing sickness cases |
| Fleet Vehicle Insurance | 3 months before expiry | Check separately for vehicle changes |
| Cyber Insurance | 3 months before expiry | Often still new contracts, terms vary widely |

These deadlines are the usual standard values – the exact terms are always stated in your policy, the affiliation contract, or the general terms and conditions (AVB).

## Trick 1: It's Not the Issue Date That Counts, But the Expiry Date

A common mistake: people go by the date the policy was signed, instead of the contract's actual expiry or renewal date. Always check the "contract expiry" or "renewal" field – with multiple policies, a central overview of all expiry dates is worthwhile.

## Trick 2: Send Your Cancellation with Proof of Delivery

Sending a cancellation by regular mail without proof is risky – if it arrives late or gets lost, your company bears the burden of proof in case of doubt. Use registered mail or A-Post Plus with tracking, and archive the cancellation letter and shipping receipt centrally.

## Trick 3: Never Cancel the Old Policy Before the New One Is Confirmed

Especially with mandatory insurance like accident insurance (UVG), there must be no coverage gap between the old and new contract – the company remains obligated to insure its employees. Wait for written confirmation from the new company before cancelling.

## Trick 4: Involve the Workforce Early for BVG

The most important point for businesses: cancelling a BVG affiliation contract requires the consent of the entire workforce or the employee representation body – simply informing them is not legally sufficient. Without this consent, the cancellation is invalid, and in the worst case the company itself becomes liable in a pension event. Plan several extra weeks for this consent process – the 6-month deadline is tighter than it sounds.

## Trick 5: Know and Use Your Special Cancellation Rights

Besides ordinary cancellation, there are situations in which a contract can be cancelled early:

- After a claim – usually within two weeks of the benefit being paid out.
- If the insurer or collective foundation raises premiums or costs.
- For BVG: if the collective foundation raises administrative costs by at least 10% within three years, the company is exceptionally entitled to a shorter one-month notice period.

## Trick 6: Report Business Closure on Time

With accident insurance (Suva or a private UVG insurer), closing a business requires at least 14 days' advance notice. This is also often forgotten during restructurings or business closures.

## Trick 7: Don't Confuse Employee Changes with Contract Cancellation

Employees joining, leaving, or changing their workload are ongoing changes, not contract cancellations – these must be reported promptly, but independently of the major notice periods. A late report can cause coverage problems in the event of a claim.

## Trick 8: Demand Cancellation Confirmations and File Them Centrally

After every cancellation, a written confirmation with the exact cancellation date and any premium refund should be requested. With multiple policies and contacts, a central filing system accessible to all relevant people is worthwhile – especially when responsibilities change within the company.

## Our Advice

With business insurance, it's usually two things that cause problems: missed deadlines and a lack of internal coordination – especially with BVG. Anyone who knows the notice periods and involves the workforce early has significantly more room to negotiate and more time for a proper comparison.

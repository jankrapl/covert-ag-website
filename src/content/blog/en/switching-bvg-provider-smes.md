Anyone who never reviews it often leaves optimization potential on the table without realizing it. Here are the key points for a switch.

## Why it pays to take a look at your BVG solution

Collective pension foundations can differ significantly in:

- Administrative costs – these directly reduce your employees' returns,
- Investment strategy and interest on accumulated capital,
- Benefits in case of disability and death,
- Funding ratio and financial stability of the collective foundation.

These differences directly affect how well your employees are covered – and how much the company pays for it.

## The notice period is the sticking point

Unlike many property insurance policies, switching your BVG provider isn't possible on short notice. Affiliation contracts with collective foundations usually run with a six-month notice period to year-end – so anyone wanting to switch as of January 1st generally has to give notice by June 30th. The exact deadline is stated in the affiliation contract and can vary slightly.

**Our recommendation:** Start the comparison process as early as spring if a switch by year-end is an option. This leaves enough time for quotes, comparison and internal coordination.

## The workforce must consent

A point that's consistently underestimated: following a Federal Supreme Court ruling, canceling a BVG affiliation contract requires the consent of the entire workforce or the employee representation body – simply informing employees is not enough. Without this consent, the cancellation is invalid, and in the worst case, the company itself becomes liable in a pension event.

For SMEs, this means concretely: the switch is not purely a management decision, but a process that must be communicated and documented early.

## Switching when premiums or costs increase

If a collective foundation raises the contributions that are not credited to savings (such as for risk and administration) by at least 10% within three years, it must announce this in writing six months in advance. In this case, the company is exceptionally entitled to a shorter 30-day notice period – a detail many SMEs don't know about and therefore never use.

Switching your BVG provider isn't something you finish in a few days – which is exactly why it pays to start early. Those who start in spring keep all their options open by summer. Those who only start in autumn often lose an entire year.

Here are the key deadlines for your private insurance policies – plus the tricks that really help in practice.

## Deadlines at a Glance

| Insurance | Typical Deadline | Special Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health Insurance – Basic (KVG) | By November 30 | Switch as of January 1; new insurer must accept you |
| Health Insurance – Supplementary | Usually 3 months before expiry | Legally separate from basic insurance |
| Household Contents Insurance | 3 months before contract expiry | Special cancellation possible after a claim (2 weeks) |
| Personal Liability Insurance | 3 months before contract expiry | Often bundled with contents insurance, can be cancelled separately |
| Vehicle Insurance | 3 months before expiry | Caution when changing vehicles during the term |
| Building Insurance | Varies by canton | Mandatory with the cantonal institution in many cantons |

These deadlines are the usual standard values – the exact terms are always stated in your policy or the general terms and conditions (AVB).

## Trick 1: It's Not the Issue Date That Counts, But the Expiry Date

A common mistake: people go by the date the policy was signed, instead of the actual expiry or renewal date. These can differ, especially if a policy was adjusted during the year. Always check the "contract expiry" or "renewal" field on your current policy.

## Trick 2: Send Your Cancellation with Proof of Delivery

Sending a cancellation by regular mail without proof is risky – if it arrives late or gets lost, you bear the burden of proof in case of doubt. Use registered mail or A-Post Plus with tracking, and keep a copy of the cancellation letter and the shipping receipt.

## Trick 3: Never Cancel the Old Policy Before the New One Is Confirmed

Especially with mandatory insurance like health insurance or vehicle liability, there must be no coverage gap. Wait for written confirmation from the new company before cancelling the old policy – even if the deadlines get tight.

## Trick 4: Watch Out for Automatic Contract Renewal

Most insurance contracts automatically renew for another year if not cancelled in time. A calendar reminder three to four months before the known expiry date prevents the deadline from slipping by unnoticed.

## Trick 5: Know and Use Your Special Cancellation Rights

Besides ordinary cancellation, there are situations in which a contract can be cancelled early:

- After a claim – usually within two weeks of the benefit being paid out.
- If the insurer raises the premium.
- If you move abroad.

A regular move within Switzerland or simple dissatisfaction with the service, on the other hand, do not count as grounds for special cancellation – the ordinary notice period still applies.

## Trick 6: Treat Basic and Supplementary Insurance Separately

With health insurance, this is the classic pitfall: basic insurance (KVG) and supplementary insurance (VVG) are legally completely separate. Cancelling the basic insurance doesn't automatically cancel the supplementary insurance too – and anyone who cancels the supplementary insurance separately risks a health check if they take out a new policy later.

## Trick 7: Look Closely When Changing Vehicles

If you sell your vehicle or buy a new one, your existing vehicle insurance doesn't end automatically – it usually needs to be transferred, adjusted, or cancelled separately. Clarify this directly with your insurer to avoid double premiums or an unwanted coverage gap.

## Trick 8: Demand a Cancellation Confirmation

After every cancellation, you should request written confirmation from the insurance company – including the exact cancellation date and any premium refund. Without this confirmation, you have no proof in a dispute that the cancellation was received and accepted.

## Our Advice

Most problems with cancellations aren't caused by complicated rules, but by simple missed deadlines. Anyone who notes the relevant deadlines early – ideally three to four months before the contract ends – regains control instead of being caught out by the calendar.

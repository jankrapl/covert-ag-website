Here are the key points for 2026.

## The Maximum Amount for 2026

How much you're allowed to contribute depends on whether you're affiliated with a pension fund:

- With a pension fund: maximum CHF 7,258 per year
- Without a pension fund (e.g. self-employed individuals or people below the BVG entry threshold of CHF 22,680 annual income): maximum 20% of earned income, up to a maximum of CHF 36,288

The full amount can be deducted entirely from taxable income – depending on canton and income, this quickly adds up to tax savings of several hundred to over a thousand francs per year.

## New for 2026: Closing Gaps Retroactively

Until now: once missed, always missed. Since 2026, you can retroactively pay into contribution gaps from the last ten years – provided you were employed in Switzerland and subject to AHV contributions, and the gap arose from 2025 onward (older gaps cannot be paid retroactively). These catch-up payments are possible in addition to the normal annual maximum and are also fully tax-deductible.

This especially opens up the possibility for people with irregular income – for example after a break for family reasons, a sabbatical, or in years with lower earnings – to make up for past gaps in a tax-effective way.

## When to Pay In? Best Early in the Year

Whether you pay in January or December makes no difference for the tax deduction – but it does for your returns. Anyone who pays in at the start of the year benefits from a full year of interest, or, with a securities-based solution, from market performance. Over 20 to 30 years, this difference can add up to several thousand francs.

**Practical tip:** A standing order prevents the December payment from being forgotten – payments made at a bank counter often need to be made before Christmas to still count for the current year.

## Multiple Accounts for a Later Withdrawal

When Pillar 3a is withdrawn – at the latest at retirement – the capital is taxed as a lump sum, separately from other income, but at a reduced rate. Anyone who spreads their savings across several 3a accounts (rule of thumb: another account is worthwhile from around CHF 50,000 per account) can stagger withdrawals over several years and so break the tax progression. Important: the annual maximum applies per person, not per account – it can simply be split and paid into several accounts.

## Not All Pillar 3a Accounts Are the Same

Whether a classic pension account or a securities-based solution makes more sense strongly depends on your investment horizon. Anyone with 15 or more years left until retirement often gives up return potential with a pure savings account. That said, the right strategy always depends on your personal risk capacity and risk tolerance – a blanket "securities are always better" falls short.
